The biting December wind whipped around the Ratlam platform, carrying the metallic tang of train tracks and a prickle of unease down my spine. It was past 10 PM, and my train to Jaipur, a critical escape route back to university and impending semester exams, was nowhere to be seen. Thirty minutes late. Every tick of my watch echoed the mounting panic in my gut.

Minutes bled into an hour, then another. Platform announcements crackled incoherently. Finally, a garbled message confirmed my worst fear: the train wasn't coming to Ratlam. It would bypass us, stopping only at Kota Junction.

Kota. A city I'd never been to, a detour that would add hours to my already strained schedule. But there was no choice. I scrambled onto the next connecting train, a local creaking towards Kota, my phone battery blinking a menacing 10%.

Reaching Kota Junction past midnight felt like stepping into a different world. The vastness of the station swallowed me whole. My train to Jaipur was supposed to depart from some platform, but which one? No phone, no internet, just me and a labyrinthine railway station at 1 AM.

I wandered, disoriented, from platform to platform, my backpack heavy with textbooks I probably wouldn't touch. Finally, I stumbled upon an information counter, a beacon of hope in the fluorescent-lit chaos. But the man behind it delivered the blow: the train had already left. Platform 5. Ten minutes ago.

The world tilted. I was stranded in Kota, penniless for all practical purposes, with no way to reach Jaipur or contact anyone. Sleep-deprived, exam-panicked, and utterly lost.

That's when I met him. An elderly man, noticing my visible distress, approached. "Kya hua beta?" he asked gently.

I poured out my predicament, my voice cracking with frustration and exhaustion. He listened patiently, then smiled. "Don't worry. There's another train early morning. You can wait here, or…" he hesitated, "…my house is nearby. You can rest there, have some food, and I'll drop you back before your train."

In any other circumstance, I would have politely declined. Stranger danger and all that. But something about his kind eyes and calm demeanor made me trust him. Besides, what choice did I have?

His home was modest, warm, filled with the aroma of home-cooked food. His wife, equally kind, heated up dinner for me. They asked about my studies, my life, offered genuine concern, and gave me a bed to sleep in.

The next morning, true to his word, he drove me back to Kota Junction well in time. I thanked him profusely, barely able to articulate my gratitude. He just waved me off. "Safe journey, beta."

I made it to Jaipur. Aced my exams. But I never forgot that night in Kota. In a moment of vulnerability, a stranger became my savior. No expectations, no conditions, just pure, unfiltered kindness.

I often wonder about that man. I wish I'd asked his name, taken his number, kept in touch. But maybe that's the beauty of it. Some people enter your life for a fleeting moment, leave an indelible mark, and disappear into the vastness, like guardian angels you'll never see again.

If you're reading this, kind sir from Kota, thank you. You restored a cynical college kid's faith in humanity.
